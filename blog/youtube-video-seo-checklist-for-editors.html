Why editors own packaging

You can deliver a perfect timeline and still lose clicks with a vague title. On many teams, the editor is the last person who understands the video’s promise - so a light SEO pass before handoff (or upload) is part of the craft, not “marketing fluff.”

The checklist

  • ☐ Primary keyword / topic is clear in the title (without stuffing)
  • ☐ Title leads with the viewer outcome (learn X, fix Y, see Z)
  • ☐ Thumbnail is readable at phone size (face/object + 2–4 words max)
  • ☐ Description opens with a useful summary in the first 2–3 lines
  • ☐ Timestamps / chapters for videos longer than a few minutes
  • ☐ Captions uploaded or burned in for accessibility and retention
  • ☐ Tags cover synonyms and common misspellings (supporting only)
  • ☐ End screen / cards point to the next logical video
  • ☐ Filename and project notes match the final title (for your archive)

Titles that promise an outcome

Weak: “Studio Vlog 47.” Stronger: “How We Lit This Product Shot (Softbox Setup).” Put the searchable topic near the front when it still reads naturally. Avoid ALL CAPS clickbait that the video cannot deliver - retention will punish you.

Descriptions that help humans

  1. First lines: what the video is and who it is for.
  2. Then: chapters, links, gear list, or related videos.
  3. Finally: soft CTA (subscribe, download, next watch).

Write for skimmers. Search engines follow helpful structure; they do not need keyword walls.

Frequently asked questions

What is YouTube video SEO?

Making a video easy to understand and discover with clear titles, descriptions, thumbnails, chapters, captions, and content that matches search intent.

Do YouTube tags still matter?

Less than title, thumbnail, and watch time - but tags still help with related terms and misspellings. Use them as support, not the main strategy.
