How to Write YouTube Titles That Rank and Get Clicks

A strong YouTube title does two jobs: it matches search intent and earns the click. Here is a repeatable formula creators and editors can run on every upload.

What a YouTube title must do

YouTube ranks and recommends videos that people understand and finish. Your title is the first filter. It should:

  • Name the topic a viewer would search or recognise
  • Promise an outcome (learn, fix, compare, see behind the scenes)
  • Match the thumbnail so the package feels honest
  • Stay readable when truncated on phones

If the title and video disagree, click-through may spike once - then retention tanks and recommendations dry up.

The outcome-first formula

Use this structure and swap parts:

[Outcome or method] + [topic] + [qualifier]

  • Outcome: How to / Fix / Build / Stop / Improve
  • Topic: the searchable subject
  • Qualifier: year, skill level, tool, time, or “without X”

Examples of qualifiers that work: in 10 minutes, for beginners, After Effects, 2026, without a microphone.

Lead with the outcome when possible. “How to colour grade skin tones in Premiere” beats “Premiere tutorial colour grade skin tones how to” every time.

Mistakes that kill CTR

  • Vague titles - “New video”, “Update”, episode numbers alone
  • Keyword stuffing - unreadable lists of search terms
  • ALL CAPS clickbait that the edit cannot deliver
  • Title/thumbnail mismatch - different promises in each
  • Too clever - puns that hide the topic

Faster drafting workflow

  1. Write a one-paragraph brief: topic, audience, promise, tone.
  2. Draft 3–5 titles with the formula above.
  3. Pick the clearest outcome that still sounds human.
  4. Check mobile truncation - front-load the important words.
  5. Align the thumbnail text (if any) with the same promise.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

Roughly 40–70 characters for mobile readability. Clarity beats stuffing.

Should keywords go at the start?

Near the front when it still reads naturally - outcome-first usually wins.

How many title options should I write?

Three to five from the same brief, then choose the clearest honest promise.
