What editors actually need

Most “best AI music” roundups target hobbyists making full songs. Editors ask different questions:

  • Can I get a 30–90s instrumental bed that matches the scene mood?
  • Can I generate whooshes, hits, and UI taps as well as music?
  • Can audio land in After Effects or Premiere without five export hops?
  • Are commercial terms clear enough for client work?

Selection criteria

  1. Instrumental control - force beds without vocals when needed.
  2. Duration control - short cues beat 3-minute songs for social.
  3. SFX support - music-only tools leave gaps at every cut.
  4. Cost visibility - know credit cost before you generate.
  5. License clarity - keep records; do not assume platform-safe by default.

In-panel vs browser generators

Browser AI music apps can sound great. The friction is context switching: generate → download → import → discover you need another take → repeat.

In-panel tools keep prompt → preview → place beside the cut. That matters when you are spotting music under dialogue and iterating every few minutes.

When stock libraries still win

Artlist, Epidemic Sound, and similar catalogues remain excellent when you need searchable moods, stems, and familiar license paperwork. AI generators complement them for:

  • Oddly specific briefs (“calm documentary piano, no drums, 22 seconds”)
  • One-shot SFX that are not in your library
  • Temp scores before a composer pass

Keyweaver does not claim to replace music licensing libraries wholesale - use the right tool for the job.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI music generator for video editors?

Prefer tools that fit your NLE and license needs. In-panel generators like Tambourine help when you need beds and SFX beside the cut; stock libraries still win for large catalogues.

Can AI music replace Artlist or Epidemic Sound?

Not always. Many editors use AI for custom cues and stock for searchable licensed catalogues.

Is AI-generated music safe for YouTube?

Only if the provider’s terms for that clip allow your use case. Read the license and keep project records.
